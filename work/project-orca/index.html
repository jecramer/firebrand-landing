Project Orca: finding growth where competitors saw a bidding war

When rising acquisition costs began undermining the economics of merge-2 games, I challenged the assumption that the answer was simply more efficient user acquisition. The underlying opportunity was to identify high-value players whose spending potential was not being captured by existing products.

Commercial context

Merge-2 was a nascent, hyper-competitive subgenre. Merge Friends launched globally in the spring of 2021 and generated €1m of revenue within its first few weeks, but every studio watching the category wanted to plant a flag before a leader emerged. Within a couple of months, twenty to thirty competing titles were bidding for the same audience on the same networks, and cost per install rose sharply.

Merge Friends retained a large, well-monetising audience but could no longer grow. Acquisition costs were out of control, and extensive client refactoring was needed just to add new features and live-ops events.

Problem or opportunity

The underlying problem was monetisation depth, not marketing spend. Merge Friends generated roughly $4 in revenue per download; Merge Mansion, the category leader, roughly $8. That gap let Merge Mansion outbid Skunkworks for the same install and still keep a healthy margin. Merge-3 games such as Merge Dragons and EverMerge earned far more again.

Strategic thesis

If the highest-value player in a $4 merge-2 game was the same person as the highest-value player in a $38 merge-3 game, the revenue gap was not about who she was. It was about what the games gave her to do.

Solsten data across three audiences, the Merge Friends player base, a merge-2 custom audience and a merge-3 custom audience, showed the same highest-LTV persona in all three. Skunkworks’ own data showed these players spent roughly three times as long in merge-2 as in merge-3, despite merge-3 earning far more per download. She was already playing merge-2 intensively; she just wasn’t being asked to spend like a merge-3 player.

  • 45% of players matching the profile named matching and merging as their favourite game type.
  • She wants visible progress and to feel productive.
  • She wants control and creativity, not one funnelled path.
  • She likes collaborating with other players without depending on them.
  • She is drawn to “realistic fantasy”: travel, a garden of her own, rescuing animals.

The hypothesis: a broadly appealing merge-2 game with real metagame depth and scalability could reach the top position not just of merge-2, but of the merge genre as a whole.

My contribution

As founder and CEO, I led the search for a more defensible growth opportunity and challenged the assumption that the answer was simply more efficient user acquisition. Project Orca became the proving ground for the repeatable identify, analyse, validate and build cycle the studio wanted to industrialise.

The persona research, concept development, covert testing and MVP build were carried out by the Skunkworks team.

Execution

From persona to proof

The team audited the themes and mechanics of games already popular with the target audience, then ran a structured brainstorm in three layers: game concepts grounded in audience data, more than sixty settings and themes, and more than sixty player fantasies. Seventeen concepts were developed in detail, with drawbacks and production risks recorded alongside their strengths.

Each was scored against nine explicit criteria: audience fit, concept fit, the centrality of human relationships or mystery, competitive positioning, branding and IP potential, opportunities for social features, scalability without a content treadmill, production workload and overall strengths and weaknesses. The strongest ideas in six thematic clusters became three composite finalists: Island Paradise, Shop of Wonders and Queendom.

Covert market testing

To keep the insight away from larger competitors, the entire testing programme ran under a shell publisher identity that could not be traced to Skunkworks, in test markets and on platforms not tracked by Sensor Tower or AppMagic. Full store-screenshot sets for each finalist carried different value propositions, characters and environments, and paid burst campaigns measured IPM and conversion. The cycle took four to six weeks, entirely before any production code was written.

Eleven weeks to a live MVP

With Island Paradise validated, the studio went from the first line of production code to a live MVP test in the Netherlands within eleven weeks. The build was fully server-authoritative, so content could ship without client updates and live operations could run remotely. The stated target was the ARPU of the highest-performing titles in the merge genre.

A mature title in this category testing on iOS in the US would typically need around 60% or more day-one retention to be considered a genre leader. Reaching the mid-fifties from a first live Android MVP, four months after research began, was read as confirmation that the theme, persona targeting and value proposition were right, not as a finished number.

Commercial implications and lessons

The commercial logic was about bid capacity. Revenue per download sets how much a studio can pay for each install at a given return on ad spend. At roughly $4 against the leader’s $8, Merge Friends could not win installs without sacrificing margin. A merge-2 game monetising with merge-3 depth would have reversed that position, allowing the studio to raise the bid ceiling on incumbents rather than be priced out by them.

Project Orca became Secret Shores, the final game Skunkworks developed. It was set up to grow by monetising more heavily than its competitors, and it remains the biggest missed opportunity of my career: it did not reach full commercial release before Skunkworks entered bankruptcy in 2022.

The method outlived the studio. AI now makes research, concept development and testing considerably faster, but it does not remove the need to validate. When execution becomes cheaper, the bottleneck shifts towards judgement: choosing the right problem, recognising an underserved audience and establishing genuine demand before committing resources.
